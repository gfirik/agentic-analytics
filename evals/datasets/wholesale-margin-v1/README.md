# wholesale-margin-v1

A **synthetic** food wholesaler's invoice lines (July 2025 to June 2026, GBP), built to prove M15's margin pack on data with a cost column. No real public dataset with a cost column passed the licence check (docs/plans/m15.md, "A real dataset with a cost column"), so margin is proven here and on hand-computed fixtures. It is reported as its own group and is not part of A1's pooled role accuracy.

- **File:** `files/invoice-lines.csv`, 518 invoice lines, one table. Run it with `bun run aa analyze evals/datasets/wholesale-margin-v1/files --yes --currency GBP --today 2026-07-03`.
- **Expected figures:** `expectations.yaml` (generated by `build.ts`, re-derived in `test/wholesale-margin.test.ts`). Later PRs' tests read it.
- **Generator:** `src/wholesale-margin.ts`, deterministic from integer pence with a fixed seed (`WHOLESALE_MARGIN_SEED = 1`, mulberry32). Rebuild with `bun run --filter @agentic-analytics/eval-datasets build:wholesale-margin`; a test fails when the committed files differ from a fresh build.

## Columns and their intended roles

Roles are those of docs/specs/semantics.md section 2. The table's kind is `sales_lines`.

| Column | Example | Intended role | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Invoice Date` | `2026-03-14` | `transaction_date` | ISO dates, 12 months |
| `Invoice No` | `INV-10001` | `invoice_id` | one invoice per customer and day; a text id with repeats, not unique per row |
| `Customer` | `Harbour Bistro` | `customer` | 12 customers |
| `Channel` | `Direct` | `channel` | `Direct`, `Distributor`, `Online`; fixed per customer |
| `SKU` | `SKU-100` | `product_id` | 8 SKUs; a code, not a quantity |
| `Product` | `Olive Oil 5L` | `product` | one name per SKU |
| `Category` | `Oils` | `product_group` | `Oils`, `Grains`, `Pantry`, `Beverages` |
| `Qty` | `64` | `quantity` | whole units, always positive (no returns in v1) |
| `Unit Price` | `38.00` | `unit_price` | list price per unit |
| `Std cost` | `27.00` | `unit_cost` | **the header a name rule alone could misread**: "Std cost" is a unit cost, not a total cost (`cost_amount`). The value pattern settles it: Qty x Std cost is a plausible line cost, and Net Amount minus Qty x Std cost is the margin |
| `Discount` | `97.28` | `discount` | money, not a percentage; 0.00 for customers without a contract discount |
| `Net Amount` | `2334.72` | `amount` | net of the discount: `Qty x Unit Price - Discount` holds on every line to the penny (a test checks it) |

Revenue is the sum of `Net Amount`. Cost is the sum of `Qty x Std cost`. There is no tax, currency or status column.

## Planted events

Prices and costs are per SKU and month. Units carry about +-3% noise each month. Revenue stays near 57,000 pounds in every month (inside +-3.5% of the month before).

1. **A margin squeeze at flat revenue (2026-03).** From March the unit cost of two products steps up and stays up: SKU-100 Olive Oil 27.00 to 31.50 and SKU-140 Sunflower Oil 30.00 to 33.50. Prices and volumes do not react. Revenue moves -0.57% (57,868.24 to 57,537.40) while gross margin falls 16,414.24 to 14,172.40 (-2,241.84, -13.7%) and margin % falls 28.36% to 24.63%. A margin pack must report the margin move, and a revenue-only report must not see it.
2. **A price rise outrunning its cost rise (2026-05).** SKU-110 Basmati Rice 10kg goes from 30.00 to 32.40 (+8%) and its unit cost from 21.00 to 21.63 (+3%); units fall about 4%. Gross margin rises 14,126.14 to 14,944.34 (+818.20).
3. **A distractor (2026-04).** SKU-170 Coffee Beans sells about 2% more units than its noise would give (+3.8% units, +3.0% revenue in the month). It sits inside the noise band and is not an event.
4. **A near-tie that is not one.** In the latest month (2026-06) the lowest margin % is SKU-100 Olive Oil at 14.06%; SKU-140 (17.78%) and SKU-160 Tea Bags (17.64%) are more than 3.5 points above it.

### Checked by hand

- **Squeeze, cost effect.** Cost = -(sum of q1 x (c1 - c0)). Only two products changed cost: SKU-100 sold 222 units in March, 222 x (31.50 - 27.00) = 999.00; SKU-140 sold 258, 258 x (33.50 - 30.00) = 903.00. Cost = -(999 + 903) = **-1902**.
- **Price event, cost effect.** Only SKU-110 changed cost: it sold 280 units in May, 280 x (21.63 - 21.00) = 176.40. Cost = **-176.40**.
- **Price event, price effect.** SKU-110's own price effect is 280 x (8861.40 / 280 - 8788.20 / 299) = 8861.40 - 8229.75 = 631.65: the list price rise (+2.40 on 280 units = 672.00) less the net-price dilution from that month's discount mix. The other seven products add 668.39 between them, all from the discount mix of their lines (a product's net unit price moves when a bigger-discount customer buys more), so the total is **1300.0378**. The effect is real and exact under the formula, but only part of it is the planted price rise.
- **Identity.** Squeeze: -26.2943 + 12.3059 - 325.8516 - 1902 = -2241.8400 = 14,172.40 - 16,414.24. Price event: -220.7209 - 84.7169 + 1300.0378 - 176.40 = 818.2000 = 14,944.34 - 14,126.14.

### Checked by a separate calculation

A throwaway DuckDB query over `files/invoice-lines.csv` (monthly sums, then the per-product volume, mix, price and cost terms written as SQL) reproduces every monthly figure above and both columns of the PVM table to four decimals. The committed test re-derives the same figures in code that shares nothing with the generator.
