A finding says *that* something changed; a decomposition says *where*. Every method is exact: the effects add up to the change, and the identity is tested on randomized cases.

## Contribution (`contribution.v1`)

For a total T = Σ xᵢ, the change ΔT = Σ Δxᵢ and each member's share is Δxᵢ / ΔT. Shares can exceed 100% when members offset each other — in the example, one customer is 108% of the drop because others grew:

## Price–volume–mix (`pvm.v1`)

When price and quantity columns exist, revenue R = Σ pᵢqᵢ splits into

- **volume** = (Q₁ − Q₀) · P̄₀,
- **mix** = Q₁ · (Σ sᵢ₁ pᵢ₀ − Σ sᵢ₀ pᵢ₀),
- **price** = Σ qᵢ₁ (pᵢ₁ − pᵢ₀),

plus separate **new**, **lost** and **unpriced** effects for items present in only one period. Volume + mix + price + new + lost + unpriced = ΔR exactly.

## Margin price–volume–mix (`pvm_margin.v1`)

When the data also has a cost column (a unit cost, or a cost amount), the same split runs on gross margin, with one more effect for unit cost. With unit margin uᵢ = pᵢ − cᵢ and ū₀ the base average unit margin:

- **volume** = (Q₁ − Q₀) · ū₀,
- **mix** = Σ qᵢ₁ uᵢ₀ − Q₁ · ū₀,
- **price** = Σ qᵢ₁ (pᵢ₁ − pᵢ₀),
- **cost** = −Σ qᵢ₁ (cᵢ₁ − cᵢ₀),

plus **new**, **lost** and **unpriced** effects for items present in only one period or without a quantity. Volume + mix + price + cost + new + lost + unpriced = ΔM exactly, also when returns make the base quantity 0. A margin that falls at flat revenue is a unit-cost story, and this split says so with numbers; without a cost column the margin analyses are not run.

## Rate vs mix (`ratemix.v1`) and the Simpson guard

For a rate r = Σ wᵢ rᵢ (a refund rate, a conversion rate), the change splits into a **mix** effect (segment weights moved), a **rate** effect (rates within segments moved), and entering, exiting and unattributed effects. When the overall rate moves against most segment rates, a `simpson_risk` flag makes the finding tell the segment-level story.

Worked numbers for every method are in `docs/specs/analysis-library.md` §4 and its tests.
