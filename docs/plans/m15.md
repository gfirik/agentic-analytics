# Plan: M15 Business semantics and the KPI scorecard

**Source:** docs/plans/analyst-grade.md §5 "M15", with the designs in §2.1 (roles, table kinds, business model), §2.2 (KPI pack, margin, ABC/Pareto, concentration), §2.3 (margin PVM), §3 (architecture) and §4.7 (launch bars). Built under the autonomy rule (STATE, 2026-10-21) and the token budget (AGENTS.md §9): one PR at a time, the spec-reviewer and QA subagents (on Sonnet) only for engine, numeric or security PRs, no real-model runs outside the nightly slot.

**Goal:** the engine knows what kind of business table it is reading and what each column means, and uses that to compute, in code, the KPIs, margin and concentration a senior analyst would check first. It shows them as a scorecard, and it says which analyses it could not run and why. M15 is the last milestone before the launch release run (M21).

## Rules that hold for every number in M15

- **The model never does arithmetic.** Every KPI, margin, margin-PVM effect, Pareto class, top-N share and HHI value is computed by a SQL template (`.sql.ts`, parameterized) or a pure function in `packages/analysis`. Each one is a fact with a `computation` record (`sql` with its parameters, or `fn` with its input fact refs).
- **Every new function has a V2 rule** in `packages/verify/src/recompute.ts`, and its hand-computed test also runs through V2. A function without a rule is a V2 mismatch (agent-and-verifier §7), so a missing rule fails the job, not the review.
- **Provenance:** measured facts cite their rows (V5 finds them again with the metric's own row query); function facts cite their inputs. A margin claim must read a confirmed or assumed cost role (new V4 check, analyst-grade §3.4).
- **The model's part stays narrow:** it may break a tie between role candidates the code produced (and only among them), and it may add business-specific metrics next to the code-drafted pack. It never proposes a role the code didn't, and it never computes a value.
- **Graceful absence:** when the roles or table kinds an analysis needs are missing, the analysis is not run and the report lists it under "Analyses not run" with its reason. It never guesses.

### A1 in detail: the role labels

- **File:** `evals/roles/labels.yaml` (Zod schema, loaded by the eval runner). It holds one entry per dataset, table and column, with `role`, an optional `accept` list of other roles that are also right, and the table's `kind`. The 30 generated businesses share two header layouts (generate.ts:228 and the `Costs` sheet), so their labels are written once per layout with the list of datasets that use it. The harness expands them per dataset, and a test checks that each dataset's real headers match its layout. Every column of every dataset is counted, as the acceptance says.
- **Who labels, and when:** one session writes the labels in PR 3 from the raw files (headers and values) and the role vocabulary in the spec. The detector doesn't exist yet, so the labels can't be fitted to it.
- **Independent review (like M14's golden review):** a separate subagent that has not seen the labels' rationale re-labels every column from the raw files and the vocabulary alone. Disagreements are settled by a third, fresh subagent. A column it can't settle gets both roles in `accept`, and the review notes why. Each entry records `reviewed_by: "agent (autonomous mode)"`, `date` and `agrees`. Labels are agent-reviewed, not maintainer-reviewed, until the maintainer re-reviews, and every result says so.
- **What "right" means:** the detector's top proposal (before confirmation, with no model: ties go to the higher score, then column order) is the label's `role` or in its `accept` list. A column whose label is `none` is right when the detector proposes no role.
- **Reporting, so the 30 can't hide a weak real-data score:** the pooled per-column rate is the acceptance figure (about 424 columns, 360 of them from the 30). Beside it the harness prints the real datasets alone (about 64 columns) and the distinct (layout, column) count. A real-only rate below 95% is recorded under "Known gaps carried forward" even when the pooled bar passes.
- **Table kinds** are labelled and scored the same way and reported, but carry no acceptance bar (the source sets none).
- **The model's tie-break** is measured separately, as information, on the nightly slot (below).

### A4 in detail: capability tags and the baseline

- PR 3 adds a `capability` field to golden items (`kpi`, `margin`, `pareto`, `driver`, `other`; accuracy.md §1). Existing items are tagged by rule:
  - `kpi`: the value of a KPI in a period, or its change against a previous or same-last-year period;
  - `margin`: anything about margin, including the "no cost column" traps (retail G8, hotel G8);
  - `pareto`: shares of the top members or of a class, and concentration.
  
  The independent golden reviewer checks the tags.
- **New items** (written in PR 3, before any M15 engine code, by one session; reviewed like M14's: a separate subagent re-derives each answer with its own SQL). The plan is about 11 items, so golden v1 grows from 32 to about 43 (the maintainer's cap is "about 40", §6.3; decision below):
  - **margin**, on the new margin dataset, 4 items (3 dev, 1 held-out): gross margin in a month, gross margin % in the latest month, the cost effect of the margin PVM in the squeeze month, and the product with the lowest margin %;
  - **margin trap** on Montgomery County (no cost column), 1 item;
  - **pareto**, 4 items: the number of customers in class A (80%) over Online Retail II's last 12 months, the top-5 supplier share in a year and whether supplier concentration (HHI) rose or fell (Montgomery County), and the top-3 agent share (hotel bookings);
  - **kpi**, 2 items: average order value in a month (Online Retail II), and the cancellation rate in the latest month against the same month a year earlier (hotel bookings).
  
  About 30% of the new items go to held-out files, as in M14.
- **Sources:**
  - A new item's `source` names a fact label from the label scheme fixed in the spec (PR 1), or `ask`.
  - A trap whose right answer is "can't be computed" may use the new source kind `not_run`. It passes when the report's "Analyses not run" lists the named analysis with a reason (accuracy.md §2: this is a structured refusal of that analysis, not a caveat beside claims).
  - Existing items keep their question, expected answer and source. Re-pointing an existing `ask` item to a report section is allowed only when the question maps to a standard section (analyst-grade §4.2). It happens in PR 3, before the engine code, and is checked by the reviewer.
- **The M14 baseline:** in PR 3, with main still at the M14 engine, `bun run eval -- --suite golden --model baseline` scores the M15 item set (dev split). The per-capability rates are committed to `evals/results/m15/0-baseline-m14-engine.json|.md` together with the engine commit (golden results gain an `engine_commit` field in PR 3). The bar per capability is `max(that rate + 15 pp, 85%)`. The expected result: the M14 engine has no scorecard, margin or Pareto facts, so every capability is below 70% and the bar is 85% for each. The number is measured, not assumed.
- **Granularity:** a capability with few counted dev items moves in big steps (with 6 items, 5/6 = 83% misses 85%). The table prints the counts beside the rates, and nothing is rounded up.

### PR 4: Role detector and the roles eval (engine, numeric)

- **`packages/profile`: `detectRoles(ws, table)`** returns candidates per column, each with a score and evidence:
  - name matches from a synonym list (EN/RU/UZ, in code, cited in the spec);
  - the inferred type;
  - value patterns (dates on or after an issue date → due-date candidate; integers with few distinct values → quantity vs code);
  - cross-column identities checked in SQL (`.sql.ts`, parameterized):
    - `qty × unit_price ≈ amount` on ≥ 95% of non-null rows, with a per-row tolerance of half a minor unit or 0.5%;
    - `amount − qty × unit_cost` gives a margin candidate only with a cost role;
    - `due ≥ issue` on ≥ 95% of rows.
- Stored in a workspace table next to the profile (a migration).
- **The model:** only a tie-break among the code's candidates when the top two scores are within a stated margin, behind the `ModelClient`, validated against the candidate list; tested with FakeModel. With no model, or with `--yes`, the top score wins.
- **Tests:**
  - hand-computed identity rates on a fixture: 19 of 20 rows match → 95% → accepted; 18 of 20 → 90% → rejected;
  - the synonym list in each language;
  - a column with no evidence → no role.
- **`aa eval --suite roles`** (`bun run eval:roles`), with a hand-computed accuracy test on a 6-column fixture.
- **Demo:** `bun run eval:roles -- --only generated`, and with the real data fetched, the whole run, pasted by QA.

### PR 8: Margin and margin PVM (engine, numeric)

- **Gross margin, drafted by code** when a cost role exists:
  - `gross_margin` = revenue − cost, where cost is Σ qty × unit_cost, or a cost-amount column;
  - `gross_margin_pct` = gross_margin ÷ revenue × 100, a ratio metric, so it is sliced by slicing its numerator and denominator (analyst-grade §2.2) and recomputed by V2.
- Margin by product, customer and channel.
- **`pvm_margin.v1`** (extends `pvm.v1`, `packages/analysis/src/decompose.ts:106–166`). With unit margin uᵢ = pᵢ − cᵢ, Q = Σqᵢ, sᵢ = qᵢ/Q and ū₀ = Σ sᵢ₀ uᵢ₀:
  - Volume = (Q₁ − Q₀) · ū₀;
  - Mix = Q₁ · (Σ sᵢ₁ uᵢ₀ − ū₀);
  - Price = Σ qᵢ₁ (pᵢ₁ − pᵢ₀);
  - Cost = −Σ qᵢ₁ (cᵢ₁ − cᵢ₀).
  
  Volume + Mix + Price + Cost = Σ qᵢ₁uᵢ₁ − Σ qᵢ₀uᵢ₀ = ΔM exactly. New, lost and unpriced or uncosted items are separate effects, as in `pvm.v1`.
- **Hand-computed case (in the spec and the test):**
  - item A: q 10→12, p 5→5, c 3→4;
  - item B: q 10→8, p 10→11, c 6→6;
  - M₀ = 10·2 + 10·4 = 60, M₁ = 12·1 + 8·5 = 52, ΔM = −8;
  - Q₀ = Q₁ = 20, ū₀ = 3;
  - Volume = 0, Mix = 20·(0.6·2 + 0.4·4 − 3) = −4, Price = 8·1 = 8, Cost = −(12·1) = −12;
  - sum: 0 − 4 + 8 − 12 = −8.
- **Runs** when a quantity role, an item dimension and a cost role exist; otherwise "Analyses not run: no cost column" (or no quantity).
- **V2 rules:** `gross_margin` (a − b), `gross_margin_pct`, and `pvm_margin.v1`, which reads its inputs in groups of six (r₀, q₀, cost₀, r₁, q₁, cost₁).
- **V4:** a claim naming margin must read a cost role that is confirmed or assumed.
- **Tests:** A2 and A3 above; a tampered effect fails V2; a margin claim on a table with no cost role fails V4.

### PR 9: ABC/Pareto, top-N concentration and its trend (engine, numeric)

- **`pareto.v1`** per dimension of an additive metric, over the trailing 12 months (the whole range when shorter) and the 12 before:
  - members sorted by value (ties by key);
  - the cumulative share curve;
  - classes A ≤ 80% < B ≤ 95% < C, where a member is in the class its cumulative share reaches;
  - the count and value of each class;
  - members with a value ≤ 0 are left out of the curve and counted as their own fact.
- **Top-3, top-5 and top-10 shares**, and the HHI per period with its trend through `trend.v1` (Theil–Sen + Mann–Kendall), which reuses V2's `share`, `hhi`, `theil_sen` and `mann_kendall`. New V2 rules: `top_n_share` and `pareto_class` (the class count is recomputed from the member facts).
- **Single-customer dependence finding:** the top member's share rises past 30% (crossing in the window, with a rising HHI trend at p < 0.05) or the top member is shrinking. The "also overdue" condition of analyst-grade §2.2 waits for M16's receivables pack.
- **The `concentration(metric, dimension, from, to)` planner tool:**
  - a fixed template;
  - the dimension must be a confirmed dimension of the metric;
  - periods are validated and passed as parameters, never interpolated.
  
  It lets `aa ask` answer share questions with facts.
- **Tests:**
  - Hand-computed: members 50, 30, 10, 6, 4 (total 100) give cumulative shares 50, 80, 90, 96, 100 → A = {50, 30} (2 members, 80%), B = {10} (90% ≤ 95%), C = {6, 4}; top-3 share = 90%; HHI = 0.25 + 0.09 + 0.01 + 0.0036 + 0.0016 = 0.3552.
  - A trend fixture with a known Theil–Sen slope.
  - The tool refuses an unconfirmed dimension.

## Risks and how each is handled

- **The 30 share two header layouts**, so 360 of about 424 columns can inflate A1. The harness prints real-only and distinct-layout rates beside the pooled one, and a real-only rate below 95% is a recorded gap.
- **No clean real dataset has a cost column.** Margin is proven on a committed synthetic dataset and hand-computed fixtures. PR 2 searches for a real one; failing that, the §4.7 real-coverage gap for margin is recorded for M21.
  - **Search result (PR 2, 2026-10-23, time-boxed under 20 minutes): none added.** Checked: (1) the analyst-grade §4.1a shortlist again: none has a cost, unit-cost or COGS column (Montgomery County has retail sales, transfers and warehouse sales only; it joins as the no-cost-column trap). (2) Microsoft's Northwind and AdventureWorks sample databases (MIT licence, read at github.com/microsoft/sql-server-samples/license.txt): they have a unit cost (`StandardCost`) and a price, but they are fictional data, not real business data, and are relational (several tables), so they fail the "real, one file" rule and would add a second synthetic source. Not used. (3) SEC EDGAR Financial Statement Data Sets (cost of revenue and gross profit per company): the page did not answer from this machine, and the data is company-level quarterly aggregates with no quantity or product, so it cannot test line-level margin or margin PVM anyway. Not pursued. (4) Kaggle sets with a profit column (Superstore and similar): a "sample data" licence the publisher does not state, and the host needs a login. Rejected under the §4.1a licence rule. Result: margin's real-data coverage stays a known gap carried to M21, and the report says margin was proven on synthetic data only.
- **Golden overfitting.** Items, tags, label scheme and the M14 baseline land before the engine code (PRs 1–3), and about 30% of the new items go to held-out files. The golden reviewers re-derive answers without the authoring SQL.
- **A bigger metric set hurts ranking** (the M14a failure mode: more families push P2/P3 out). PR 7 dedupes by definition and groups families by definition, the regression set guards recall@5, and the scorecard is its own section rather than more findings.
- **Identity checks on messy data** (cancellations, rounding, returns). Per-row tolerances are stated in the spec; negative quantities and zero prices fall to the unpriced effect, as in `pvm.v1`; an identity below 95% is evidence against a role, never a hard rule.
- **A confirm screen that grows hard to read.** Roles show only where they matter (columns with a role), with evidence in one line; `--yes` keeps working unattended.
- **Small item counts per capability** make 85% coarse. Counts are printed beside rates and nothing is rounded up.
- **Montgomery County's host may block us** (EIA-861M did). It is DEFERRED with its command, and Pareto keeps its real coverage from Online Retail II and the hotel's agents.
- **Laptop limits.** The roles eval, full suite and real tier are heavy jobs, run one at a time through `scripts/heavy.sh`.

## Decisions (most spec-consistent option; recorded in STATE as [M15 plan])

1. **Role vocabulary:**
   - dates: `transaction_date`, `issue_date`, `due_date`, `paid_date`, `period`, `snapshot_date`;
   - ids: `order_id`, `invoice_id`, `line_id`, `customer_id`, `product_id`, `supplier_id`;
   - dimensions: `customer`, `product`, `product_group`, `channel`, `location`, `supplier`, `salesperson`, `dimension`;
   - money and measures: `amount`, `quantity`, `unit_price`, `unit_cost`, `cost_amount`, `discount`, `tax`, `refund_amount`, `paid_amount`, `balance`, `stock_on_hand`, `count`, `measure`;
   - other: `status`, `currency`, `description`, `none`.
   
   This covers every role analyst-grade §2.1 names, so M16–M17 add packs, not roles.
2. **Table kinds** are analyst-grade's six (sales lines, invoices, bills, inventory snapshots, bank/cash, budget/plan) plus `period_summary` (already aggregated per period: the 30's `Costs`, NZ card spend) and `other`. Without the two extra kinds, those tables would be forced into a wrong kind.
3. **Labels** live in `evals/roles/labels.yaml`. They are written before the detector exists, checked by an independent re-labelling subagent, and allow an `accept` list where two roles are both right. They are agent-reviewed, not maintainer-reviewed.
4. **A1 is the pooled per-column rate**, as the source writes it, over the real datasets plus the 30 (the committed coffee-roaster-v1 and the margin dataset are reported separately). It is measured on the detector's proposal before confirmation, with no model. Real-only and distinct-layout rates are printed beside it, and a real-only miss is a known gap.
5. **A4's "M14 baseline"** is the scripted M14 engine scored on the M15 item set in PR 3, before any M15 engine code, because the only published M14 baseline is scripted (the real-model one is still DEFERRED). The real-model check is the absolute 85%, paired with `golden-v1` only when that run used an M14 engine.
6. **Capability bars** are computed over counted dev items only. Held-out items are scored in release runs (M21), as in M14.
7. **The `not_run` source:** a trap answered by the report's "Analyses not run" entry passes. It is a structured refusal of that analysis.
8. **Golden v1 grows to about 43 items** (from 32), over the maintainer's "about 40" cap (§6.3), so that every M15 pack has ≥ 3 golden items (§4.7). It stays near the cap by adding no items outside margin, KPI and Pareto.
9. **Montgomery County** (second wave, §4.1a) is added in M15 for real supplier concentration and a third real layout for A1. M16 reuses it for inventory flow. NHSBSA, HMRC and Seoul bikes stay with M16+.
10. **The concentration trend** is the HHI and top-N shares per period, through `trend.v1`. Single-customer dependence fires when the top share rises past 30% with a rising trend, or when the top member is shrinking. The "overdue" condition comes with M16.
11. **The Pareto window** is the trailing 12 months (or the whole range when shorter) and the 12 before. The classes are at 80% and 95% cumulative, over positive members only. Ties go by key.
12. **Margin:** cost is Σ qty × unit_cost, or a cost-amount column. `gross_margin_pct` is a ratio metric, so it slices by numerator and denominator. Contribution margin waits for variable-cost data (no M15 dataset has it) and is listed under "Analyses not run".
13. **Reviews:** PRs 4–10 (engine or numeric) get the spec-reviewer and QA subagents on Sonnet; PRs 0–3 and 11 (docs, eval data, eval tooling, results) get `scripts/gate.sh` and green CI only (AGENTS.md §9).
